Dear Professor Katherine Denby,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "The Sorghum Lipid Database (SoLD): population-scale lipidomics linking environmental and genetic variation in the Sorghum Association Panel," for consideration as a Resource article in The Plant Journal.

Lipids sit at the intersection of membrane biology, energy storage, and stress signaling, yet population-scale plant lipidomics resources remain scarce — particularly ones linked to natural genetic variation in field-grown crops. Sorghum is an attractive system for closing this gap given its climate resilience and the depth of genomic resources now available for the Sorghum Association Panel (SAP), including the whole-genome resequencing of the panel published in The Plant Journal by Boatwright et al. (2022). We see SoLD as a natural extension of that resource for the lipidomics community.

Using high-resolution LC–MS, we quantified 244 lipid species across the SAP grown under two contrasting field regimes and identified reproducible, population-wide compositional signatures associated with the low-input environment, including triacylglycerol enrichment, phosphatidylserine-centered phospholipid rebalancing, and coordinated lysophospholipid remodeling reflected in altered LPC/LPE ratios. These shifts were supported across complementary analytical layers — class-level abundance contrasts, lipid ontology enrichment, and acyl-chain chemical-space summaries — indicating a coordinated redistribution across interconnected lipid pools rather than isolated changes in a few classes. GWAS of individual species, class sums, and class ratios identified recurrent loci with environment-specific functional themes: basal lipid metabolism, chloroplast biology, and cell-wall organization under control conditions versus nutrient-status signaling, cell-wall remodeling, defense, and cold-related barrier formation under low input. Integration with LINEX-based reaction-network analysis further identified candidate enzymes (LCAT-like, PNPLA1/3, LRO1, DGAT1) consistent with the observed compositional shifts.

The manuscript shows results from two different field seasons under two different agronomic conditions: low input and control conditions. We address this throughout the manuscript by interpreting all contrasts conservatively as between-trial differences rather than as causal effects of the low-input regime, and we explicitly identify same-year common-garden experiments and targeted candidate validation as the necessary next steps. We believe the population-wide reproducibility of the lipidome shifts (broad effect-size distributions, perfect leave-one-out sign stability, and convergence across compositional, ontology-based, and chemical-space analyses) makes the resource and the candidate-locus catalog valuable to the community despite this caveat.

All datasets, analysis pipelines, and results are freely available through an interactive Shiny application (https://github.com/rr-lab/SAP-Lipidomics-Database) that supports hierarchical filtering by lipid class and subclass, multiple data representations (raw, TIC, CLR), and integrated browsing of GWAS results and recurrence-based gene summaries.
This manuscript has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration by another journal. All authors have approved the submission and declare no competing interests. A preprint is available on bioRxiv.

We hope you will find the work suitable for The Plant Journal and look forward to your decision.
