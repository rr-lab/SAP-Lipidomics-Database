\begin{abstract}
Sorghum (\textit{Sorghum bicolor}) is a climate-resilient crop whose acclimation to nutrient limitation and low temperature likely involves extensive lipidome reconfiguration. Lipids are key membrane components, carbon and energy stores, and mediators of stress signaling, yet population-scale lipidomics data for sorghum are limited. We present the Sorghum Lipid Database (SoLD), a curated lipidomics resource from the Sorghum Association Panel grown under two field regimes, (i) a nutrient-sufficient with usual planting date environment (control) and (ii) a low-input treatment with reduced nitrogen and phosphorus, earlier planting, and no pesticide application (low-input). Using high-resolution LC--MS, we quantified 266 lipid species and found the lipidome broadly similar across trials, with four low-input-associated signatures relative to control, (i) depletion of sulfoquinovosyldiacylglycerol, (ii) triacylglycerol enrichment, (iii) phospholipid redistribution centered on phosphatidylserine, and (iv) a shift in the lysophosphatidylcholine-to-lysophosphatidylethanolamine ratio. Genomic heritability was zero at the median under control against 0.171 under low input, so more of the lipidome could be mapped under low input. GWAS of lipid species, class sums, and class ratios recovered loci that were largely trial-specific, with candidate genes assigned by linkage disequilibrium rather than physical distance and prioritized by LD-aware ontology enrichment and reaction mapping. Control-associated loci yielded twenty enriched gene sets, all confined to triacylglycerol traits and led by cutin biosynthesis, whereas low-input-associated loci yielded fifty-one across five lipid classes, centred on nutrient transport and signaling and on sphingolipid metabolism, and including a chromosome~3 block carrying two LOW PHOSPHATE ROOT homologues. SoLD thus connects sorghum lipid diversity to environmental and genetic variation, and lets users move from lipid class to individual species and associated candidate loci. It is freely accessible at \url{https://nirwan.shinyapps.io/SAP-Lipidomics-Database/}.




\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Plants routinely encounter environmental stresses that reduce yield, including nutrient limitation, low temperature, and disease. Nitrogen (N) and phosphorus (P) deficiencies constrain growth and nutrient allocation, while cold disrupts membrane properties and metabolism \citep{Schluter2012, Silveira2018, Emendack2021, Ding2019, Guo2025}. Lipids are key to these responses as membrane components, energy reserves, and signaling precursors. Stress-induced lipid remodeling affects membrane stability, photosynthesis, storage lipids, and lipid signaling \citep{Bohn2007, Siebers2015, Hou2016, Rawat2021, Li2020, Bhattacharya2022, Xu2025}. Lipids also contribute to plant defense against pathogens, particularly, cuticular and membrane lipids form physical barriers to infection \citep{KuzniakGajewska2024}. In sorghum, fungal diseases such as anthracnose, grain mold, charcoal rot, downy mildew, and rust can substantially affect yield and grain quality, making disease pressure an important component of field stress \citep{Khaskheli2025SorghumFungal}. Under P limitation, phospholipids are often replaced by non-P galactolipids and sulfolipids such as DGDG and SQDG, whereas cold can change acyl-chain composition, phospholipid turnover, and triacylglycerol (TG) accumulation \citep{Essigmann1998, Hartel2000, Jouhet2004, KellyDormann2002, Tan2018, Pfaff2020, Yan2025, Zhang2020}. These shifts are coordinated by nutrient and stress pathways, including SPX--PHR phosphate signaling, nitrate transport (NRT2/NPF), sulfate transport for plastid sulfur supply, and enzymes driving phospholipid turnover and DG--TG cycling \citep{Lv2014SPX4,Lupini2016NAR2,Sorgona2011Nitrate,Xia2015NPF24,Chen2019SULTR3,Bose2021NPC,Ngo2019NPC,Tan2018,Kelly2011SDP1L,Yen2008DGAT}. However, crop-scale lipidomics linking lipid remodeling, nutrient stress, biotic stress, and natural variation is still limited, especially in sorghum.

Sorghum is a stress-tolerant cereal with extensive diversity. The Sorghum Association Panel (SAP) captures broad botanical, geographic, and genomic variation for studying environmentally and agronomically important traits \citep{Casa2008CommunityResourcesSorghum, Boatwright2022}. Because population lipidomics is sensitive to technical and field variation and depends on consistent annotation, we applied SERRF normalization, SpATS spatial correction, and GNPS-assisted annotation to improve reproducibility and interpretation \citep{Fan2019, RodriguezAlvarez2018, Wang2016GNPS, Molenaar2019}.

Interpreting large candidate gene sets from lipid GWAS is challenging because LD-linked genes are not independent. A single associated region may contain multiple neighboring, similarly annotated genes, inflating enrichment. GWAS-focused tools (INRICH, SNP2GO, Gowinda) mitigate this by accounting for LD, SNP structure, and gene-level biases \citep{Lee2012INRICH,Szkiba2014SNP2GO,Kofler2012Gowinda}. Accordingly, we tested GO enrichment using an LD-aware interval-permutation null and separated terms supported by multiple independent intervals from those driven by single loci.

We present the Sorghum Lipid Database (SoLD), a curated lipidomics and GWAS resource for the SAP. Leaf samples (V6--V8) were profiled in two field trials, a 2019 control (CTL; adequate nutrients, standard planting) and a 2022 low-input regime (LIN; reduced N and P, earlier planting to induce cold-associated stress, no chemical management). Because CTL and LIN were grown in different years, contrasts are treated as between-trial differences, not causal treatment effects. SoLD integrates LC--MS lipidomics, compositional analysis, lipid ontology, GWAS of species- and class-level traits, LD-aware GO enrichment, LINEX reaction mapping, and an interactive Shiny app. This framework identifies four LIN-associated signatures, SQDG depletion, TG enrichment, PS-centered phospholipid redistribution, and altered LPC/LPE balance, and prioritizes candidate functions spanning nutrient acquisition, DG--TG cycling, phospholipid turnover, cold response, and jasmonate signaling. Each part of the LIN regime is matched by candidate loci, with nitrate transporters for lower nitrogen, phosphate-status signaling proteins for lower phosphorus, a cold-induced transcription factor block for the earlier planting, and jasmonate-pathway genes for the absence of chemical treatment, alongside both sides of DG--TG cycling, including \textit{DGAT1}. SoLD supports exploration of sorghum lipid variation, candidate loci, and accessions for functional validation; the app is available at \url{https://nirwan.shinyapps.io/SAP-Lipidomics-Database/}.

\section{Results}

\subsection*{QC and normalization yielded comparable LC-MS performance across conditions}

We profiled early-stage lipids from SAP grown in two field conditions defined earlier. The workflow (Supplementary~\ref{fig:suppfig_s1_workflow}) comprised tissue collection and extraction, high-resolution LC-MS lipidomic profiling, GNPS-assisted lipid annotation, post-acquisition data correction with SERRF and SpATS \citep{Fan2019, RodriguezAlvarez2018}, and data dissemination via the SoLD Shiny app (\url{https://nirwan.shinyapps.io/SAP-Lipidomics-Database/}).

Run-order quality control showed stable analytical performance (Supplementary~\ref{fig:suppfig_s2_qc}A--B), with the total ion current (TIC) and the summed signal intensity per sample meeting expected values across system checks, internal standards, and blanks. TIC variability among QC samples was low (7.5\% CV for CTL and 6.0\% CV for LIN). SERRF normalization improved technical precision, reducing median QC-RSD from 3.6\% to 2.0\% in CTL and from 1.6\% to 0.3\% in LIN. The proportion of features with QC-RSD below 30\% rose from 75.7\% to 94.1\% and from 77.3\% to 89.3\% respectively (Supplementary~\ref{fig:suppfig_s2_qc}C--D).

PCA of normalized data showed tighter sample clustering than unprocessed data (Supplementary~\ref{fig:suppfig_s2_qc}E--F). After spatial correction, the leftover variation in MGDG(18:3/18:3) is scattered randomly across the field rather than forming rows, columns, or patches, indicating that position in the field no longer drives the signal (Supplementary~\ref{fig:suppfig_s2_qc}G--H). Thus, the post-correction lipid signal distributions remained similar across cohorts, were used for all subsequent analyses.

\subsection*{Genotypic lipid variation is largely independent of botanical race, and heritable variation is confined to LIN}

SAP is structured by botanical race and geographic origin \citep{Casa2008CommunityResourcesSorghum, Boatwright2022}. Next, we tested whether lipid variation is affected by race or marker-based genetic clusters ($K=6$; group sizes in Supplementary Table~S1). Using Kruskal–Wallis with BH correction within trials (Supplementary Table~S2), we found little association. No lipid-class sum was significant for race in either trial. The genetic cluster was significant only for PS under LIN ($q=1.16\times10^{-6}$, $\varepsilon^2=0.104$; Fig.~\ref{fig:fig1_population_structure}). PS is carried by a single species under LIN, PS(18:0/18:2), so the class sum and that species are the same measurement. The same class-level test under CTL, where PS is carried by two species, was not significant ($\varepsilon^2=0.020$, $q=0.358$). Furthermore, none of the species-level tests were significant under CTL, and nine were significant under LIN, eight of them by genetic cluster alone and one, 11,14,17-eicosatrienoic acid, by both genetic cluster and botanical race (Table~\ref{tab:species_ancestry}; Supplementary Table~S4). PCA of class composition likewise showed minimal structure, with groupings explaining $\le$1.6\% of variation (Supplementary~\ref{fig:suppfig_s3_class_pca}; Supplementary Table~S3). Thus, ancestry is not a confounder.

Subsequently, we quantified genetic variance in the lipidome by estimating genomic heritability ($h^2$) for every species (214 under CTL, 216 under LIN) and compared the trials on the 164 common species measured across the same 351 genotypes (Supplementary Table~S5). Under CTL, the median $h^2$ was near zero, and only 11 of those species had heritability that we could distinguish from zero. Under LIN, the median was 0.171, and 54 species did. The same direction held for species by species comparison, with $h^2$ higher under LIN for 132 species, higher under CTL for 21, and equal for 11 (Supplementary~\ref{fig:suppfig_s4_heritability}), and for class by class comparison where every class was higher under LIN except TG and PA (Supplementary Table~S6). Estimating $h^2$ directly on the 17 class sums gives similar results, with a median of 0 under CTL compared to 0.117 under LIN. 15 of the 17 classes are higher under LIN (paired Wilcoxon $p=0.007$), with the same two exceptions. Only four class sums have an interval excluding zero in either trial (Table~\ref{tab:classsum_h2}; Supplementary Table~S7). Thus, aggregation does not recover heritability, and the trial difference represents a shift in the whole distribution rather than a handful of strongly heritable classes. The contrast is not an artifact of ancestry or of which genotypes were sampled. It survives conditioning on the three leading eigenvectors of the relationship matrix, dropping each botanical race in turn, and twenty random 80\% subsamples of the panel, with LIN exceeding CTL in every run (Supplementary Tables~S6 and S8). Nor does it follow from noisier measurement under CTL. Across the same 164 species, total phenotypic variation was comparable between trials (median coefficient of variation 0.28 under CTL against 0.30 under LIN, paired Wilcoxon $p=0.29$), and the per-trial estimates drew on more accessions under CTL than under LIN (389 against 357).

Moreover, heritability increases unevenly across classes (Table~\ref{tab:species_heritability}). Under CTL, it is largely confined to TGs, which account for 19 of the 67 TG species and 19 of the 20 heritable species in that trial. Under LIN, it concentrates in the plastid membrane lipids, with 4/5 MGDG, 6/10 SQDG and 4/7 DGDG, the highest proportions of any class with more than one species. MGDG and SQDG also carry the largest absolute shifts in \%TIC of any class apart from TG, falling by 2.6 and 1.5 percentage while TG rises by 2.3, indicating that both compositional and genetic changes are centered on the photosynthetic membrane lipidome. TG carries heritable species in both trials, though not of the same kind. Every one of the 19 heritable CTL TG species contains an acyl chain of 14 carbons or fewer and sits below 0.05\% of TIC, and 14 of the 20 heritable CTL species have a coefficient of variation above one, against 2 of the 66 heritable species under LIN. The CTL set therefore behaves closer to detection or non-detection than to graded abundance, while the 25 heritable LIN TG species are long-chain and vary within the range of the rest of the lipidome. PS, PA, and LPE each include a single species, so they reflect that species rather than the class.

The near-absence of heritable species under CTL is consistent with either less genetic variance or a larger residual share, and because neither trial was replicated, we have no independent estimate of plot error to separate the two. Genotype, therefore, accounts for far more of the leaf lipidome under LIN than under CTL, which is the asymmetry that the GWAS results below follow.

\subsection*{Lipidome overview for CTL and LIN SAP}

We quantified a total of 266 lipid species, with 164 detected in both trials, 50 only in CTL, and 52 only in LIN (Supplementary~\ref{fig:suppfig_s5_counts}). All lipid values for both trials are browsable in the SoLD Shiny application. TGs and PCs were the most diverse classes in both environments (TG: 67 in CTL, 66 in LIN; PC: 36 in CTL, 38 in LIN; Supplementary~\ref{fig:suppfig_s5_counts}; Supplementary Table~S15). The largest contributors to between-genotype variance are the most abundant species in both trials, led by MGDG(18:3/18:3) and PC(16:0/18:2) (Supplementary Table~S10).

Furthermore, we summarized lipid abundance as \%TIC, so the values represent the relative signal in each condition. In both trials, the glycerolipids and glycerophospholipids dominated the annotated signal (CTL: 65.4\% and 30.3\%; LIN: 64.3\% and 30.7\%), while the prenol lipids were the only other category above 1\% (3.4\% and 4.2\%) (Fig.~\ref{fig:fig2_class_composition}A). The sterol lipids show the largest proportional change of any category, rising 5.1-fold from 0.04\% to 0.22\% of the annotated signal, almost all of it \textit{$\beta$}-sitosterol.

Within the galactolipids and phospholipids, MGDG contributed the most with 32.7\% (CTL) and 30.1\% (LIN), while PC contributed 24.2\% and 23.3\%. DGDG (12.1\%, 12.2\%) and DG (13.0\%, 13.8\%) followed. TG increased from 2.6\% (CTL) to 4.9\% (LIN), while SQDG decreased from 2.8\% to 1.3\%. Overall, these thirteen classes comprised 94.7\% (CTL) and 93.5\% (LIN) of annotated signal. The remainder is carried by species with no shorthand class name, chiefly prenol lipids (3.4\% and 4.2\%) and fatty acyls, which Fig.~\ref{fig:fig2_class_composition}B shows under their LIPID MAPS category (Supplementary Table~S11). Class-level contrasts between trials on the centered and additive log-ratio scales are given in Supplementary Table~S12. These class shares do not depend on panel composition, the largest class moving by at most 0.21 percentage points under race-dropping and repeated 20\% subsampling (Supplementary Table~S16).

Additionally, two minor pools show large changes masked in the bulk profile. PS increases from 0.014\% to 0.045\% of annotated signal, and its correlation with PC flips between trials ($r=0.45$ in CTL vs. $r=-0.34$ in LIN; Supplementary Table~S13; Supplementary~\ref{fig:suppfig_s6_pcor}). Among lysophospholipids, the two classes move in opposite directions. LPE rises from 0.006\% to 0.014\% of annotated signal while LPC falls on both log-ratio scales (CLR $-0.14$, ALR $-0.62$; Supplementary Table~S12), so the LPC/LPE ratio halves from 23.2 to 11.1. Both pools stay below 0.2\% in both trials.

Within each trial, PCA of the major glycerolipid and glycerophospholipid pools showed lipid class to be the main driver of species-level variation (Supplementary~\ref{fig:suppfig_s7_pca}). TG species formed a distinct cluster separated from most membrane lipids in both trials, though along different principal axes, while galactolipids and glycerophospholipids partly overlapped but occupied class-enriched regions, supporting class-level aggregation for the compositional and reaction-network analyses that follow.

We next asked whether the class shifts were accompanied by shared structural and biophysical properties using LION, a lipid ontology that groups species into such categories \citep{Molenaar2019}. LION ranking analysis across the shared lipid species returned ten significant terms, five higher and five lower with respect to LIN (\(q<0.05\); Fig.~\ref{fig:fig2_class_composition}C; Supplementary Table~S17). Higher in LIN were \textit{triacylglycerols}, \textit{lipid storage} and \textit{lipid droplet}, all three annotated on the same 44 species, together with \textit{headgroup with neutral charge} and \textit{glycerolipids}. Lower in LIN were \textit{the membrane component}, \textit{low lateral diffusion}, \textit{lipid-mediated signaling}, and \textit{PC(38:2)} and \textit{C20:1}, the last two relying on too few annotated lipids to interpret (Supplementary Table~S17). The five higher in LIN all report the same thing, that storage lipid increased, rising from 16.9\% to 19.7\% of annotated signal. The three lower in LIN are from the membrane side. \textit{Membrane component} is the bilayer itself, and it falls with the shared phospholipids, from 30.2\% to 28.5\% of annotated signal, almost all of it PC (24.2\% to 21.9\%). \textit{Low lateral diffusion} is the tightly packed, ordered fraction of that bilayer, and \textit{lipid-mediated signaLINg} is the DG, PA, and lyso pool released by membrane turnover, and both are lower in LIN as well. Together, these terms suggest a leaf holding less bilayer and more storage lipid under LIN, consistent with acyl chains being routed into droplets rather than into new membrane. Of these shifts, it recovers only TG enrichment as galactolipids, and the sulfolipid is missing from the ontology. PS and lysophospholipids have too few shared species for a rank test.

Finally, we summarized each lipid class in a reduced chemical space using the abundance-weighted mean total carbon number and double-bond count across molecular species (Supplementary~\ref{fig:suppfig_s8_chem}; Supplementary Table~S18). Carbon number cleanly separates the classes, from lysophospholipids and monoacylglycerols through diacyl lipids to TG, and diacyl unsaturation varies independently of chain length. Across trials, PE shows the largest unsaturation increase among classes with more than ten species; otherwise, aside from a higher TG carbon number, every class except the non-comparable PS changes by less than one carbon and 0.25 double bonds. Within-class chemistry is therefore largely stable, and CTL--LIN differences reflect shifts in class proportions rather than chain composition.

Compared with CTL, four population-wide shifts characterize the LIN lipidome: (i) SQDG depletion, (ii) TG enrichment, (iii) a phospholipid rebalancing centered on PS, and (iv) an LPC/LPE balance shifted toward LPE. All rests on the abundance and composition analyses. LION corroborates only the TG shift. Because the trials also differ by year, field, planting date, and analytical batch, these shifts characterize LIN as a whole and cannot be attributed to just nutrient supply and cold. The reaction-network and GWAS sections that follow ask which reactions and which loci accompany them. Candidate genes below follow for the TG and SQDG shifts. The PS and lysophospholipid changes sit on pools under 0.2\% of TIC and are not resolved to candidates, although PS is the one class whose abundance tracks the genetic cluster (Fig.~\ref{fig:fig1_population_structure}C,D).

\subsection*{GWAS recovers more genomic regions under LIN and more trait-sharing per region under CTL}

We ran separate GWAS for each individual lipid trait, lipid class sum, and lipid class ratio in both trials (747 in total: 220 individual species and 153 class sums and ratios under CTL, 221 and 153 under LIN), using a univariate linear mixed model with the first three genotype PCs to account for population structure (Fig.~\ref{fig:gwas_manhattan}). SNPs surpassing the Bonferroni genome-wide significance threshold ($p \leq 8.19 \times 10^{-9}$ for CTL and $8.22 \times 10^{-9}$ for LIN) were mapped to candidate genes using the LD-based cutoff of 0.4. Under CTL, this identified 1,062 candidate genes for individual lipids and 54 for class sums and ratios (Supplementary Tables~S19 and S20). Under LIN, the same procedure identified 4,370 and 385 candidate genes (Supplementary Tables~S21 and S22).

Candidate gene counts do not equal signal counts because one significant SNP tags all genes in its LD window. After binning candidates into 250 kb windows, there were 358 distinct windows under CTL and 949 under LIN. So, LIN captures $\sim$2.6$\times$ more loci, not the four-fold suggested by gene counts. Hence, CTL loci average 3.0 candidate genes, while LIN loci average 4.6. Candidates also differ in phenotype recurrence. In the individual-lipid layer, 586 of 1,062 CTL candidates were linked to more than one phenotype compared to 1,474 of 4,370 under LIN. Thus, CTL yields fewer loci, each carrying many lipid associations, while LIN yields more loci carrying few. The class-sum and ratio layer is too small under CTL for the same contrast (Supplementary Table~S20). Because lipid phenotypes are highly correlated by design, these recurrence counts are descriptive rather than formal tests against a null; the cross-trial comparison in the next section is evaluated explicitly.

Table~\ref{tab:top_loci} lists the five loci linked to the most phenotypes in each trial and trait layer, one row per signal with the number of genes it tags. The trial difference is not just a counting artifact, since genomic heritability is also higher under LIN, and that estimate uses no LD windows or gene counts. Traits with higher heritability are easier to map. Therefore, the longer LIN candidate list follows from the heritability difference rather than confirming it.

\subsection*{LD-aware GO enrichment reduces the candidate lists to a small number of interpretable terms}

Both candidate lists were too large to inspect gene by gene, so we tested Gene Ontology (GO) biological-process and molecular-function terms for over-representation within each trial lipid class, using a permutation null that preserves LD-block structure. This distinguishes terms supported by multiple independent intervals from those driven by a single LD block.

In total, 344 of 1{,}292 tested terms passed the LD-aware null ($q_{\mathrm{LD}}<0.05$). Because GO terms are nested and class sums and ratios share candidate genes, we merged terms with identical gene sets, leaving 217 term--gene-set combinations across 159 distinct terms (Supplementary Table~S23). Of these, 87 draw on $\leq 30$ background genes, while the rest were too broad to interpret such as \textit{zinc ion binding} (909 genes) and \textit{transmembrane transport} (865 genes). Seventy-one of the 87 are supported by two or more independent intervals, while the remaining 16 rest on a single LD block. Figure~\ref{fig:go_bp_main} shows the strongest four per lipid class among the 71, giving 22 gene sets.

Those 22 gene sets are unevenly split between trials, with 18 under LIN and 4 under CTL, the same asymmetry that the candidate counts show. All four CTL gene sets come from TG traits. The LIN gene sets center on nutrient transport and signaling. \textit{Cellular response to phosphate starvation} is the strongest of them, at $51\times$ enrichment across three independent intervals, and is one of only two gene sets with more than one lipid class. \textit{Nitrate transmembrane transporter activity} follows from fatty-acid traits at $25\times$ across two intervals, and \textit{sphingolipid metabolic process} from AEG at $35\times$. Fatty acids, PC, and TG each contribute four gene sets, and SQDG contributes three. Enrichment therefore narrows the 5,176 candidate genes to the 919 that fall within an enriched term, a short list that could be used for functional validation.

\subsection*{Candidate genes overlap between trials only for individual species, and the overlap does not survive collapsing linkage}

The two trials recovered largely different candidate lists, but different GO terms cannot indicate whether the underlying genetics differ. We therefore counted how many candidate genes the two trials share and compared that count with the chance expectation from the 34{,}027 \textit{Sorghum bicolor} v3.1 gene models, both at gene resolution and after collapsing linked genes into 100, 250, and 500 kb windows (Fig.~\ref{fig:fig7_ctl_lin_overlap}). Overlap was tested with a hypergeometric test and summarized as fold enrichment over chance and as the Jaccard index, the proportion of all candidate genes appearing in both conditions.

At the gene level, the two candidate sets overlapped more than chance, with 291 genes shared against 140 expected ($2.07\times$, $p = 1.2\times10^{-35}$), but the excess comes entirely from the individual-lipid layer ($2.07\times$, $p = 2.7\times10^{-34}$; Table~\ref{tab:overlap}). The class-sum and ratio layer shares no gene at all between trials, against 0.6 expected by chance, so it is neither enriched nor depleted, only empty. The shared genes are in any case a small fraction of the total, with a Jaccard index of 0.056. Within the individual-lipid layer, the excess is carried by TG and PC ($1.74\times$ and $3.47\times$, $q<0.02$), while fatty acids overlap no more than chance, and four further classes share no genes at all, including SQDG, despite its 400 LIN candidates (Table~\ref{tab:overlap_class}; Supplementary Table~S27).

This mainly reflects LD, because each association maps every gene in its window, and gene-dense regions are mapped repeatedly in both trials. Because no window size is objectively correct for collapsing linkage, we repeated the test at 100, 250, and 500 kb and read the trend across them as the result. For individual lipids, the excess fell at every step, from $2.07\times$ at gene level to $1.17\times$ at 100 kb and $1.05\times$ at 250 kb, where it was no longer significant ($p = 0.22$), and $1.04\times$ at 500 kb ($p = 0.26$), so loci recovered in the two trials are no more concordant than two independent samples of the same genome. The class-sum and ratio layer share no window at any of the three sizes.

Recovering the same gene never meant recovering it for the same lipid. Of the 291 shared genes (Supplementary Table~S28), none are candidates for the same trait in both trials, although 13 traits have significant associations in both. At locus resolution, the two trials therefore recover essentially independent sets, and the gene-level excess reflects linkage rather than shared genetic control.

\subsection*{LINEX-based reaction mapping, reaction-balance scoring, and GWAS support}

To place lipid associations in a biochemical context, we used LINEX2 \citep{Rose2023LINEX2}, which integrates curated lipid-reaction data with measured lipid abundances to identify biochemical connections among lipids and enzymes \citep{Morgat2016Rhea}. Since LINEX2 does not provide a sorghum reference, we used rice instead. Therefore, the resulting network is considered a set of testable hypotheses. The resulting network (Fig.~\ref{fig:Fig4_linex_rewiring_abc}A) revealed four candidate branches linking DG/MG, TG, and lysophospholipid pools: (i) PC+DG$\leftrightarrow$LPC+TG (LCAT*), (ii) TG$\leftrightarrow$DG (PNPLA1-labeled lipase branch), (iii) PE+DG$\leftrightarrow$TG+LPE (LRO1/PDAT-like transfer), and (iv) DG+MG$\leftrightarrow$TG (PNPLA3-labeled branch). LINEX2 returned an enzyme assignment for three of these but not for branch~(i), so the LCAT assignment was made based on the reaction's specificity (RHEA:32843, RHEA:44236) and is marked LCAT* throughout. The substrates, products and RHEA identifiers of each branch are given in Supplementary Table~S29. These annotation-based hypotheses do not establish \emph{in vivo} flux direction, and because CTL and LIN were sampled in different years, all LIN--CTL contrasts are genotype-matched between-trial ratios rather than causal effects of management.

For each branch, we computed a per-sample reaction-balance score as the mean log abundance of products minus substrates \citep{Rose2023LINEX2, Gieger2008, Illig2010}, so a batch effect that scales every lipid by the same factor cancels. In genotype-matched paired Wilcoxon tests across 355 genotype pairs, all four branches shifted, and all four kept their direction when recomputed on only the species measured in both trials. The results were positive for PNPLA3 ($+0.314$), LRO1 ($+0.261$), and LCAT* ($+0.139$), and negative for PNPLA1 ($-0.256$) (BH-adjusted $p<10^{-52}$; Supplementary Table~S29). TG-producing branches shifted in one direction and the hydrolytic TG$\rightarrow$DG branch in the other, showing that the class-level shift holds within genotypes and not just on average. Because substrates and products are different lipid classes, scale invariance does not remove class-specific trial artifacts, so these directions remain trial differences.

Cross-referencing branches with the LIN GWAS candidate tables supported three of four (Supplementary Tables~S21, S22 and S30). Branch~(i) was supported by two LCAT-superfamily genes, \texttt{SORBI\_3006G214500} and \texttt{SORBI\_3004G341900}, whose closest Arabidopsis homologues are LCAT4 and PSAT. The characterized members of this family release a lysophospholipid from PC or PE rather than transferring the acyl group to diacylglycerol \citep{Chen2012LCATPLA, Banas2005PSAT}. Branch~(ii) supported by \texttt{SORBI\_3001G041900}, a PNPLA-domain lipase in the SDP1 clade associated with three TG species under LIN. Finally, branch~(iv) by \texttt{SORBI\_3010G170000} (\textit{DGAT1}). No candidate was found for branch~(iii), and no PDAT-family gene was recovered in either trial (Supplementary Table~S31). The same cross-reference against the CTL candidate tables returned a single gene on branch (ii), \texttt{SORBI\_3005G144300}, a patatin-domain lipase on branch~(ii) ($p = 3.10\times10^{-10}$; $r^2_{\max}=0.51$). All four of its associated traits are TG species that lie on that branch, against 3 of 14 for the seven LIN genes mapped there, and it also falls in the CTL-enriched term \textit{triacylglycerol lipase activity} ($10.7\times$ across two independent intervals; Supplementary Table~S25), so enrichment and the reaction network recover it independently. Together, these results link the class-level composition shift to four candidate reactions, three showing LIN, and one showing CTL genome-wide association.

Next are five adjacent chromosome 3 genes, each carrying a variant in perfect linkage disequilibrium ($r^2 = 1$) with the most significant SNP at the locus, recurring in the same 11 phenotypes, one class sum (free fatty acids), the species FA(22:1), and nine class ratios, sharing the same association ($p = 4.46 \times 10^{-20}$). Within the 500~kb window, the association concentrates in $\sim$7.70--7.78~Mb (Fig.~\ref{fig:Fig5_shiny_app}C). Three of the five block genes are annotated as two multicopper oxidases of the LOW PHOSPHATE ROOT family and a PHO2-family ubiquitin-conjugating E2, so the block carries genes of the phosphate-starvation response.  LD cannot distinguish among the five, and the associations are with fatty-acid traits rather than phospholipid abundances. Thus, the app lets readers explore the full candidate space by choosing their dataset, trait type, lipid class, and LD threshold; tracing any lipid to its associations and candidate genes not discussed in this paper.

\section{Discussion}
The trials differed in lipid class composition, and their lipid-trait loci were largely distinct. We relate lipidome changes to trial conditions, highlight candidate genes, and conclude with the near-absence of shared loci between trials. GWAS has produced many candidates to interpret directly. Given too many candidates, we focused on genes in ontology terms passing the LD-aware permutation null, which cover 919 of the 5,176 candidate genes and are supported by both enrichment and association (Supplementary Table~S26). We also focused on genes mapping to LINEX reaction branches for lipid related candidates. Additionally, based on the recurrent loci across multiple lipid traits (Fig.~\ref{fig:Fig5_shiny_app}B), we identified a chromosome~3 phosphate block. These three filters ask different questions and return largely different candidate genes to functionally validate.


\subsection*{Integrated interpretation of lipidome remodeling across contrasting field trials}

The two most prominent changes in LIN are a decrease in SQDG and an increase in TG. The accumulation of TG is commonly associated with low temperatures, drought, oxidative stress, and nutrient-driven carbon reallocation, suggesting a shift towards increased storage within the LIN signature \citep{Tan2018, Yan2025, Pfaff2020}. The median LIN values are higher than CTL for every lipid ratio in which TG is the numerator. Thus, TG rises against every class it was tested against (Supplementary Table~S14). DG rises less than TG but more than MG, giving DG/MG 1.3$\times$, while DG/PS falls to 0.32$\times$ because PS rises further still. Correlations also change for PS. PS--TG switches from negative in CTL ($r=-0.26$) to positive in LIN ($r=+0.43$), a strong sign reversal (Supplementary Table~S13). Additionally, LINEX reactions add to these results. The three TG-producing branches (i), (iii), and (iv) shift towards products under LIN, while TG$\rightarrow$DG hydrolysis on branch~(ii) shifts towards the substrate, collectively favoring TG over DG (Supplementary Table~S29). These class-level balances together suggest a reorganization of DG supply and TG synthesis and mobilization, though direct measures of enzyme activity are needed. Both arms carry LIN candidates. On branch~(iv) (DG+MG$\leftrightarrow$TG), \textit{DGAT1} (\texttt{SORBI\_3010G170000}) acylates diacylglycerol to triacylglycerol and falls within a repeatedly mapped chromosome 10 crude-fat QTL in sorghum \citep{Boyles2017DGAT1, Yen2008DGAT}. On branch~(ii) (TG$\leftrightarrow$DG), the reverse, hydrolytic step, the candidate is \texttt{SORBI\_3001G041900}, an SDP1-clade lipase. In Arabidopsis, SDP1 hydrolyzes triacylglycerol to diacylglycerol and free fatty acid and accounts for most TG breakdown during reserve mobilization \citep{Kelly2011SDP1L}. This locus clusters with \textit{SbSDP1-L} in the sorghum lipase phylogeny \citep{Sapara2024PLA}. The same reaction network places two LCAT-superfamily acyltransferases (\texttt{SORBI\_3006G214500}, \texttt{SORBI\_3004G341900}) on branch~(i) (PC+DG$\leftrightarrow$LPC+TG), which releases lysophospholipids from PC, although characterized members of this family release a lysophospholipid from PC or PE rather than transferring the acyl group to diacylglycerol, so the branch assignment remains a hypothesis \citep{Chen2012LCATPLA, Banas2005PSAT}.

Another change involves the balance among phospholipid headgroups. PS increased against every class it was tested against (Supplementary Table~S14), and across genotypes, the PS--PC correlation flipped from positive in CTL ($r=+0.45$) to negative in LIN ($r=-0.34$), the largest shift among class pairs. Thus, the two headgroups move together across the panel under CTL but trade off against each other under LIN (Supplementary Table~S13). PE showed the largest increase in mean double-bond number of any class, although the species detected differ slightly between trials, and this is a composition measure rather than a direct account of acyl editing. Comparable shifts in headgroup proportions, membrane unsaturation, and lysophospholipid pools are reported under cold, nutrient, oxidative, and water limitation, where they accompany changes in membrane fluidity, lipid repair, signaling, and remobilization in other studies \citep{Hou2016, Rawat2021, Yan2025, Zhang2020}. The balance between the lysophospholipid classes also shifted, with LPC/LPE dropping to 0.48-fold in LIN (Supplementary Table~S14), driven by rising LPE while LPC remained essentially unchanged (Supplementary Table~S11). PS and the lysophospholipids each contribute under 0.2\% of TIC, so these are large proportional changes on small pools. The annotation is also shallow, with two PS species under CTL and one under LIN, four LPC species under CTL and three under LIN, and a single LPE species in both, so none of these shifts can be read as evidence of phospholipase activity.

These changes do not indicate phosphate conservation, nor a headgroup swap. Under phosphate limitation, phospholipids are degraded to release phosphate and are replaced by glycolipids, raising SQDG/DGDG and lowering phospholipids \citep{Essigmann1998, Hartel2000, Jouhet2004, KellyDormann2002, Pfaff2020}. However, neither occurs here. On the CLR scale, SQDG drops the most, galactolipids drop relatively less, and PG shows no change (Supplementary Table~S12). Thus, SQDG falls while PG, the anionic thylakoid lipid it would normally replace, stays flat, and the two together fall from 3.13\% to 1.72\% of TIC. Phospholipids hold steady (30.3\% to 30.2\% of TIC) while glycolipids fall (47.5\% to 43.5\%), raising the ratio of phospholipids to glycolipids. Additionally, all phospholipids increase relative to SQDG (PC $2.07\times$, PE $2.41\times$, PG $2.54\times$; Supplementary Table~S12). PC salvage via deacylation would increase LPC, but LPC does not accumulate ($0.87\times$ CLR), so we find no evidence for that route behind the modest PC decline. Overall, the lipidome suggests a smaller plastid lipid fraction with a disproportionate loss of SQDG. Because SQDG is the only sulfur-containing membrane lipid, constrained sulfolipid synthesis is one explanation for why it falls further than galactolipids, though we did not measure sulfur and other explanations remain open. The LIN GWAS returned one gene annotated as a sulfoquinovosyl transferase SQD2 (\texttt{SORBI\_3002G000600}) and one paralogue from the same glycosyltransferase family (\texttt{SORBI\_3003G076900}), both under fatty-acid traits rather than any SQDG trait, so they are candidates to follow up rather than evidence for the sulfolipid route. Without sulfur, chlorophyll, or thylakoid measurements, the lipidome alone cannot separate these possibilities, and we therefore describe the LIN lipidome as a redistribution among plastid, phospholipid, lysophospholipid, and neutral-storage pools rather than a phosphate-driven remodeling.


\subsection*{Genetic control of lipid variation is stronger under LIN and largely trial-specific}

Heritability was low in both trials. It is a ratio, so a small value can mean little genetic variance or a large residual, and with no replication, we have no independent estimate of plot error to distinguish between the two. Our estimates also come from a marker relationship matrix, so they capture only the additive variance that markers tag and will remain below broad-sense estimates from replicated designs. Published leaf metabolomes provide some backup. Mean broad-sense heritability across 76 wheat leaf metabolites was 0.25, with genotype-by-location variance twice the genetic variance \citep{Matros2017}, and 25--40\% of maize leaf mass features fell below $H^2=0.2$ \citep{Zhou2019}. Our LIN values sit near that range, and our CTL values are below it, which the narrow-sense comparison explains in part but not fully.

The heritability difference between the trials remains consistent. The paired estimates use the same genotypes and the same species, so it is not an artifact of the panel or the species set. It is unlikely to be a power problem since CTL had more accessions, or a noise problem since total phenotypic variation was similar. The data show a smaller genetic share of variance under CTL. Without replication, we cannot tell whether this reflects less genetic variance, more residual variance, or both. This supports the idea that favorable conditions mask genetic differences in lipid composition, which emerge under stress. We did not test which LIN factor drove the effect. Ancestry explains little in either trial, and the small detectable signal appears only under LIN.

The GWAS points the same way. Under LIN, species with candidate genes had a higher median $h^2$ than those without (0.345 compared to 0.129), while under CTL, both groups were near zero. More heritable traits are easier to map, so this is a consistency check, not independent support. Likewise, the longer LIN candidate list may reflect more mappable traits, not more genes. After collapsing linkage, candidate loci are mostly trial-specific, and repeated regions do not have the same trait.

\subsection*{Control-associated loci point to cuticular and phospholipid-turnover genes}

CTL returned fewer candidates than LIN, and all 20 well-supported gene sets come from TG traits with most general cellular processes. Four name a lipid process, and one of those, \textit{triacylglycerol lipase activity}, is treated above with the reaction network. The remaining three fall into two groups, cutin biosynthesis and phospholipid turnover (Supplementary Table~S23).

\textit{Cutin biosynthetic process} is the most enriched of the twenty and is driven by \texttt{SORBI\_3001G222700} and \texttt{SORBI\_3003G360700}. \texttt{SORBI\_3001G222700} encodes a very-long-chain aldehyde decarbonylase related to rice WDA1, a wax biosynthesis protein  \citep{Jung2006WDA1} and associates with eight TG species, five palmitate-containing. \texttt{SORBI\_3003G360700} is a GPAT6-family acyltransferase. In tomato it esterifies hydroxy fatty acids to glycerol and its loss reduces cutin \citep{Petit2016GPAT6}. Because cutin and wax monomers derive from C16/C18 pools, the palmitate enrichment is consistent with shared precursors. As we measured whole-leaf lipids (not surface wax), this likely reflects precursor pools rather than cuticle composition.

The second group is phospholipid turnover. \textit{Phospholipid dephosphorylation} and \textit{phospholipid metabolic process} share LPP-family phosphatidic acid phosphatases \texttt{SORBI\_3003G078700} and \texttt{SORBI\_3010G193300}. The latter term also includes secretory phospholipase A2 \texttt{SORBI\_3005G145100}. LPPs convert PA to DG, limiting PA signaling. In Arabidopsis, \textit{LPP2} loss elevates PA and increases ABA-sensitive germination \citep{Katagiri2005LPP2}. Sorghum's drought tolerance is attributed in part to leaf wax load \citep{Busta2021, Fontanet2025SorghumDrought}, and both the cuticle and ABA signaling are under lipid control, so these five genes are worth testing under water deficit. That is a hypothesis rather than a result, since CTL received standard management with sufficient irrigation.

\subsection*{Fatty-acid traits under LIN map to a chromosome 3 block of cold- and development-linked regulators}

One LIN locus is notable for showing consistent, fatty-acid–specific associations. \texttt{SORBI\_3003G406800}, a MADS-box transcription factor on chromosome 3, is associated with FA(22:1) and PC(18:2\_20:4) and with the fatty-acid class sum plus eight FA-to-class ratios (Supplementary Tables~S21 and S22). The signal does not resolve to one gene, eight genes in a 340 kb window share the same association pattern, and \texttt{SORBI\_3003G406600} (SBP/SPL) is in perfect LD ($r^2=1$) with the lead SNP. The only LD-aware GO term is \textit{DNA binding} (from 1{,}166 genes), so interpretation relies on the associations and prior studies.

Three genes in the region have relevant literature. The sorghum MADS gene is cold-induced and a hub in an early shock co-expression module \citep{Kurt2026ColdSorghum}. Its closest maize homolog, \textit{ZmMADS69}, is a flowering-time locus involved in tropical-to-temperate adaptation \citep{Liang2019ZmMADS69}.Additionally, the maize ortholog of the SBP/SPL gene, \textit{ZmSPL6}, accelerates female flowering when knocked out under long days but not short days, implying photoperiod- and temperature-dependent effects \citep{Jin2023ZmSPL6}. Also in the window, \texttt{SORBI\_3003G407200} ($r^2=0.50$) encodes a CG-1 (CAMTA) DNA-binding domain. In Arabidopsis, CAMTAs regulate \textit{CBF2} and contribute to freezing tolerance \citep{Doherty2009CAMTA}.

LIN was planted earlier to increase cold exposure, so a window containing two cold-linked regulators associated with fatty-acid traits is consistent with cold acclimation, which remodels acyl chains to maintain membrane fluidity \citep{Tan2018, Zhang2020}. However, earlier planting also changes photoperiod and temperature during development, and the \textit{ZmSPL6} phenotype is conditional on such developmental timing, making that explanation equally plausible. However, our design cannot distinguish these effects singularly because of compounding effects. The data support only an association between this block and fatty-acid traits under LIN and the causal pathway remains unresolved.

\subsection*{Nitrate and NPF transporters link nitrogen status to fatty-acid traits under LIN}

Nitrogen transport enters the LIN candidate set via two GO enrichments. First, nitrate-specific terms (\textit{nitrate transmembrane transporter activity} and \textit{nitrate transmembrane transport}) are strongly enriched in the fatty-acid set because three of the five annotated genes are candidates. However, all three cluster on chromosome 4. Second, broad transport terms (\textit{transmembrane transporter activity} and \textit{transmembrane transport}) have large backgrounds (402 and 865 genes) and weak enrichment (1.2–2.6$\times$), providing no cargo specificity, but their genes span up to 39 intervals and recur across the fatty-acid, PC, TG, and SQDG sets, implying a dispersed, multi-locus signal.

Nitrate-associated terms map to a 250 kb region on chromosome 4 containing three NRT2-family genes, including \texttt{SORBI\_3004G009200}, \texttt{SORBI\_3004G009400}, and \texttt{SORBI\_3004G009500}. All share the closest maize homolog \textit{nrt2} (\texttt{Zm00001eb209670}) and correspond to rice \textit{OsNRT2.1}/\textit{OsNRT2.2}, high-affinity root nitrate transporters that function with NAR2.1 \citep{Yan2011OsNAR21, Lupini2016NAR21}. \texttt{SORBI\_3004G009400} associates with FA(22:1), the fatty acid class sum, and two oxidized fatty acids. The other two peak at $r^2_{\max}=0.67$ and $0.55$ and each associates with one lipid. Because the interval includes 23 LIN candidate genes, LD does not uniquely implicate the transporters.

Two additional NPF/NRT1 transporters, \texttt{SORBI\_3008G081400} and \texttt{SORBI\_3001G133900}, appear in the broad transport terms. \texttt{SORBI\_3008G081400} is recovered in both trials (seven TGs under CTL, five of which are palmitate-containing; FA(22:1), TG(18:1\_18:2\_18:2) and 11,14,17-eicosatrienoic acid under LIN). Its closest Arabidopsis homolog is NRT1.2, which transports both nitrate and abscisic acid without the two competing as substrates \citep{Kanno2013NPF46}. The same protein interacts with PHOSPHOLIPASE D$\alpha$1 at the plasma membrane, with \textit{PLD$\alpha$1} epistatic to \textit{NRT1.2} in ABA responses \citep{Li2020NRT12PLD}, placing the homolog at a junction of nitrate transport, ABA transport and phospholipase D activity, a plausible point of contact between N status and membrane lipid turnover, however, that interaction was characterized in seed germination rather than in leaves. \texttt{SORBI\_3001G133900} associates with one lipid, TG(14:0\_18:2\_18:3), and its rice ortholog OsNPF2.4 mediates low-affinity nitrate root-to-shoot transfer \citep{Xia2015NPF24}. Thus, these transporters are the most promising nitrogen-related candidates in the LIN set for follow-up work.

\subsection*{Phosphate-starvation genes and a plastid sulfate transporter associate with fatty-acid and TG traits under LIN}

Under LIN, the term cellular response to phosphate starvation is enriched across most lipid sums, and in the SQDG traits, its three genes are \texttt{SORBI\_3001G028500} on chromosome 1, and \texttt{SORBI\_3003G088800} and \texttt{SORBI\_3003G088900} on chromosome 3. \texttt{SORBI\_3001G028500} encodes an SPX-domain phosphate-status signaling protein and is associated with FA(22:1), the fatty-acid class sum, and the SQDG-to-fatty-acid ratio. Like rice OsSPX4, which negatively regulates phosphate signaling through its interaction with PHR2, it may regulate phosphate-starvation responses \citep{Lv2014SPX4}.

The two chromosome 3 genes sit in one of the most recurrent loci recovered under LIN, across 52 significant SNPs associated with FA(22:1), the free fatty acid class sum, and nine class-ratio traits. \texttt{SORBI\_3003G088800} and \texttt{SORBI\_3003G088900} are separated by 1.8 kb and are closest to the rice \textit{OsLPR2} and \textit{OsLPR1}, the multicopper oxidases of the LOW PHOSPHATE ROOT family, which control the root meristem response to phosphate deficiency. In rice, the family has expanded to five genes within 65 kb, of which \textit{OsLPR3} and \textit{OsLPR5} are induced by Pi deficiency \citep{Cao2016OsLPR}, so the sorghum pair is closest to the non-induced members. The same block carries a PHO2-family ubiquitin-conjugating E2, \texttt{SORBI\_3003G089000}. PHO2/UBC24 is a negative regulator that limits phosphate uptake and root-to-shoot transport and is miR399-cleaved under phosphate deficiency \citep{Aung2006PHO2, Huang2013PHO2}. In Arabidopsis the RING-type E3 ligase NITROGEN LIMITATION ADAPTATION partners with UBC24 to ubiquitinate PT2 \citep{Park2014NLA}, a link that matches our design because LIN reduced both N and P. Under the strictest LD setting ($r^2 \geq 0.9$) (Fig.~\ref{fig:Fig5_shiny_app}C), the block resolves to five genes, each carrying a variant in perfect linkage with the lead SNP.

Two transporters appear only under broad transport terms, not phosphate- or sulfur-specific ones (Supplementary Tables~S24 and S25). \texttt{SORBI\_3006G244000}, a STAS-domain protein assigned to the SULTR3 family, is associated with TG(14:0\_18:3\_18:3), TG(12:0\_18:2\_18:3), and PC(18:2\_20:4). In Arabidopsis, SULTR3 proteins localize to the chloroplast envelope and mediate chloroplast sulfate uptake \citep{Chen2019SULTR3}. Because SQDG synthesis depends on plastid sulfate import and reduction, SULTR3 likely acts upstream of the SQD2-family loci noted above in the SQDG pathway. Furthermore, \texttt{SORBI\_3002G116100} encodes a PHT1-family H$^+$/Pi cotransporter. Its closest characterized rice homolog is OsPT4 (\textit{OsPht1;4}), which affects phosphate accumulation and embryo phosphate mobilization \citep{Ye2015OsPT4,Zhang2015OsPht14}. However, its five associated traits are four TGs and one PC, arguing against a simple phospholipid-to-glycolipid replacement under phosphate limitation. Thus, these loci are prioritized for functional validation for P and sulphur related phenotypes.

\subsection*{Jasmonate and oxylipin genes are LIN-specific candidates}

Under biotic stress, the dominant lipid signals are oxylipins made from unsaturated fatty acids, and jasmonate, in particular, mediates defense against pathogens \citep{Kuzniak2024LipidsPathogen}. This pathway starts with lipoxygenases. In the 13-LOX branch, 18:3 is oxygenated and, after three steps, converted to jasmonate, which is then conjugated to isoleucine to form JA-Ile. JA-Ile binds the COI1--JAZ complex, promoting JAZ repressor degradation and activating defense genes \citep{Li2022JasmonateReview}. LIN enrichments recover this pathway. Seven candidate genes fall under LIN and none under CTL. Five are lipoxygenases across four loci, including \texttt{SORBI\_3001G125800} and \texttt{SORBI\_3001G483400} (chr1), \texttt{SORBI\_3003G385500} and \texttt{SORBI\_3003G385900} (26 kb apart; chr3), and \texttt{SORBI\_3008G191000} (chr8). \texttt{SORBI\_3001G483400} orthologs Arabidopsis LOX3, whereas \texttt{SORBI\_3008G191000} corresponds to LOX1 (9-LOX), spanning both branches. Three other candidates lack characterized orthologs. \texttt{SORBI\_3009G249900} is orthologous to rice \textit{OsJAR1}, which produces JA-Ile. The \textit{osjar1} mutants show light-dependent coleoptile elongation, indicating developmental as well as defensive roles \citep{Riemann2008OsJAR1}. \texttt{SORBI\_3010G130400} is annotated as a jasmonic acid carboxyl methyltransferase. The rice ortholog \textit{OsJMT1} methylates jasmonate, is induced by brown planthopper, and its overexpression increases methyl jasmonate but reduces herbivore-induced JA/JA-Ile and decreases height and yield, consistent with a growth--defense trade-off \citep{Qi2016OsJMT1}.

Given sorghum’s common fungal disease pressure (e.g., anthracnose, grain mold, charcoal rot, downy mildew, rust) \citep{Khaskheli2025SorghumFungal}, an untreated trial could possibly shift this pathway. However, we did not test for any insect damage or disease trial in LIN, so this remains a hypothesis for future work.

\section*{Conclusion}

SoLD is a population-scale lipidomics web resource for SAP, profiling 266 annotated lipid species across two contrasting fields. Compared with CTL, the LIN lipidome showed four population-wide shifts, SQDG depletion, TG enrichment, a PS-centered phospholipid redistribution, and an LPC/LPE balance shifted toward LPE. Botanical race explained none of these, and genome-wide genetic structure explained only the PS shift. Genomic heritability was higher under LIN, with a median of zero under CTL against 0.171 under LIN, so more of the lipidome can be mapped under LIN than under CTL. Because neither trial was replicated, we cannot say whether that difference reflects greater genetic variance or smaller residual variation.

GWAS produced far more candidates than could be interpreted. The candidates we take forward fall into two groups. Lipid-acting genes cover both sides of DG--TG cycling, with \texttt{SORBI\_3010G170000} (\textit{DGAT1}) on the synthetic side, an SDP1-like patatin lipase (\texttt{SORBI\_3001G041900}) on the hydrolytic side, and two LCAT-superfamily acyltransferases on the PC branch that releases lysophospholipids. Nutrient-related genes match the inputs that were withheld. Phosphate is represented by a chromosome 3 block carrying two LOW PHOSPHATE ROOT homologues and a PHO2-family E2, which shares an enriched \textit{cellular response to phosphate starvation} term with a chromosome 1 SPX-domain gene. Sulfur is represented by \texttt{SORBI\_3006G244000}, a SULTR3-family chloroplast sulfate transporter that sits on the supply route to SQDG. Nitrogen is represented by \texttt{SORBI\_3004G009400}, a high-affinity nitrate transporter driving the nitrate-transport enrichment in the fatty-acid traits. Separately, a cold-induced MADS-box regulator, \texttt{SORBI\_3003G406800}, ties the earlier planting date to fatty-acid variation.

Every candidate named so far comes from LIN. The CTL set is narrower and confined to TG traits, and its interpretable terms point to cuticular lipid synthesis, with a WDA1-related very-long-chain aldehyde decarbonylase (\texttt{SORBI\_3001G222700}) and a GPAT6-family acyltransferase (\texttt{SORBI\_3003G360700}), and to phospholipid turnover, with two LPP-family phosphatidic acid phosphatases and a secretory phospholipase A2. A patatin-domain lipase (\texttt{SORBI\_3005G144300}) is the only CTL gene on any LINEX branch, recovered independently by enrichment and by the reaction network.

Loci were mostly trial-specific, so leaf-lipid genetic control is environment-specific at the locus level in this design. We therefore recover regions, not genes. The chromosome~3 phosphate block spans 15 genes across 233~kb at the $r^2 \geq 0.4$ threshold, the chromosome~4 nitrate interval tags 23 candidates, and the MADS-box locus shares all associations with seven neighbouring genes. In all cases, the association cannot resolve the causal gene, and because many loci map to multiple lipid traits, neither the gene nor effect breadth is clear. Functional validation should target regions (e.g., multiplex edits, block deletion/complementation), reserving single-gene knockouts for loci where linkage is already broken. These are candidates, not causes; separating nutrient treatment from year and planting date will require same-year factorial trials with both regimes.


\section*{Materials and methods}

\subsection*{Plant Material and Growth Conditions}

We used the Sorghum Association Panel ($\sim$400 genotypes), a genetically diverse collection of sorghum accessions representing major botanical races and broad phenotypic diversity \citep{Casa2008CommunityResourcesSorghum, Boatwright2022}. We evaluated SAP accessions across two different field settings during two growing seasons (2019 and 2022) at the Pee Dee Research and Education Center, Clemson University, Florence, South Carolina. The ``control'' condition, herein denoted as CTL, involved standard agronomic inputs with sufficient levels of nitrogen (N) and phosphorus (P) along with a typical planting schedule. In contrast, the ``low input'' condition, denoted as LIN, featured reduced N and P coupled with earlier planting to mimic a cold stress environment.

\subsection*{Schematic diagram}

The schematic in Supplementary~\ref{fig:suppfig_s1_workflow} was created using FigureLabs.ai (\url{https://www.figurelabs.ai/}).

\subsection*{Lipidomics Analysis}

\paragraph{Sample Preparation and Extraction.}
Samples were prepared following the standard extraction protocols explained in the paper by \citep{Barnes2022}.

\paragraph{Liquid Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry (LC-MS) Analysis.}
Lipid extracts were analyzed at the Joint Genome Institute/Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory using their standard LC-MS/MS electrospray ionization lipidomics workflow; detailed chromatographic conditions, acquisition parameters, and quality-control procedures are described in the published JGI/LBNL protocol using a Thermo Exploris 120 Orbitrap mass spectrometer inline with an Agilent 1290 UHPLC system \citep{Louie2025JGI_Lipids}.

\paragraph{Feature Detection and Data Processing.}
Raw LC-MS data, in both positive and negative ion modes, were processed utilizing MZmine 2, an open-source software for the analysis of mass spectrometry data \citep{Pluskal2010MZmine2}. The pipeline encompassed peak detection, chromatogram construction, deconvolution, and isotope filtering, producing a detailed feature table containing mass-to-charge ratio-retention time (mz-rt) pairs. Isotopic peaks were excluded to minimize redundancy, as a single metabolite can yield multiple co-eluting ions, such as adducts and in-source fragments; remaining mz-rt duplicates were resolved at the annotation stage described below. We acknowledge that such degeneracy can lead to an inflated number of features compared to the actual number of metabolites present, which we considered during metabolite identification.

\paragraph{Blank/extraction-control filtering, intensity thresholds, and sparsity pruning.}

Features were removed when their maximum intensity across biological samples ($b$) was less than $10\times$ their maximum across extraction controls ($a$), unless at least one biological sample exceeded the extraction-control maximum, and a minimum average intensity of $\sim 10^{6}$ peak height was required within the treatment groups of interest. Samples with $\geq 70\%$ zero or missing features were excluded before normalization, since ultra-sparse profiles destabilize scaling and produce spurious differential signals. A lipid species counts as detected in a condition when it passes these filters using only that condition's samples; species passing in both are shared and the rest condition-specific. Full filter parameters are in the GitHub repository.

\paragraph{MS/MS spectral library matching and cross-referencing of IDs.}
For each feature analyzed by MS/MS, the most intense fragmentation spectrum was queried against the GNPS database, and features without a match were eliminated. Library matches give putative identifications (Metabolomics Standards Initiative levels 2/3) that may include isomers or near-mass analogs, so species-level assignments are putative throughout and individual annotations should be confirmed against authentic standards before being treated as identified compounds. Where a feature yielded multiple GNPS hits, the highest MQScore (cosine similarity) was retained as the primary annotation. Lower-ranked candidates were kept for verification but excluded from subsequent statistical analyses. Table-linkage keys and the full tie-breaking rule are in the GitHub repository.

\paragraph{Systematic Error Removal Using Random Forest (SERRF).}
Following the cleaning process, the data were then used for SERRF normalization \citep{Fan2019}. We used the SERRF server (\url{https://slfan2013.github.io/SERRF-online/}) to obtain the normalized output. After applying SERRF, only biological samples were preserved. Any zeros were substituted with two-thirds of the minimum nonzero value for that feature to prevent potential infinite logarithmic transformations.

\paragraph{Spatial Correction.}
Finally, we removed spatial patterns across the trials with the R package \texttt{SpATS} \citep{RodriguezAlvarez2018}, which fits a two-dimensional P-spline ANOVA surface over the field coordinates, modelling each feature's intensity as a mean plus smooth row, column, and row-by-column terms. For downstream analyses we used SpATS-adjusted intensities, the model residuals plus the fitted mean. This preserves the original intensity scale and avoids negative values, which the \%TIC, CLR, and log-ratio transforms require, and corrects positional artifacts such as edge effects. Smoothing parameters, knots, penalty orders, and full model specifications are in our GitHub repository.

\paragraph{Lipid Quantification.}
The identified lipid species were assigned to the eight LIPID MAPS categories, and to classes and subclasses within them, following the LIPID MAPS classification \citep{Fahy2009LipidMapsClass} (Supplementary Table~S9). Category is the top tier used in the figures; lipid class refers throughout to the abbreviation carried in a species name, such as PC or TG. In each sample the total intensity for a class was obtained by summing the species within it and normalized to the sample's total ion current, giving relative abundances as percentages of TIC, which were then averaged across samples within each condition.

\paragraph{Lipid ratio calculation.}
For each sample $i$ and lipid class $c$, species intensities were aggregated to class totals,
\[
C_{i,c}=\sum_{k \in c} I_{i,k}.
\]
Two annotated features were excluded from all class- and category-level comparisons. Phytosphingosine rose from 0.04\% of total signal under CTL to 16.9\% under LIN, which would make a free sphingoid long-chain base the second most abundant feature of the LIN lipidome and is not biologically plausible for a leaf. SM(d18:1/17:0) was the only member of its class, and plants do not synthesize sphingomyelin, so an odd-chain C17 species of this type is most consistent with an internal standard or misannotation. Both differences track the separate 2019 and 2022 analytical runs rather than genotype. Neither feature yielded a GWAS candidate, so no genetic result depends on this exclusion. Because log-ratios are scale-invariant, TIC normalization was unnecessary. For genetic analyses, class totals were computed from SpATS genotype BLUPs rather than plot-level intensities, to retain the spatial correction. BLUPs are deviations from the panel mean and are negative for $\sim$60\% of genotype-by-class combinations, so the class-level intercept $\bar{C}_{c}$, the panel mean total for class $c$ on the input intensity scale, was added back before log transformation ($A_{g,c}=\mathrm{BLUP}_{g,c}+\bar{C}_{c}$). This returns each genotype to a strictly positive intensity scale in both trials, so no pseudo-count, shift, or denominator floor was applied.
 Pairwise class log-ratios were then
\[
r_{g,c_1/c_2}=\log_{10}A_{g,c_1}-\log_{10}A_{g,c_2}.
\]
Because $r_{g,c_1/c_2}=-r_{g,c_2/c_1}$, each class pair yields the same trait up to sign and identical association statistics, so we kept only one ordering per pair. With 17 classes, this gives 17 class sums and $\binom{17}{2}=136$ ratios, for 153 traits per trial.

\paragraph{Compositional transforms.}
Class totals and the pairwise class log-ratios defined above are the traits carried forward to GWAS. For species-resolved analyses we used the centered log-ratio transformation, $\mathrm{CLR}(x_i)=\ln(x_i/g(\mathbf{x}))$, where $g(\mathbf{x})$ is the geometric mean of species-level abundances in the sample, which removes the constant-sum constraint of compositional data.

\subsection*{Lipid species inventory}

Species counts report every annotated feature in a trial, whether it is named in lipid shorthand, the convention that names a species by its class and acyl composition, or under a trivial chemical name, as the carotenoids, sterols, tocopherols, quinones and free sphingoid bases are. The same set is used for the species counts and for every composition analysis, so the \%TIC category values cover it in full. Class labels follow the lyso normalisation used throughout, so a species written with an empty second chain is counted as the lysolipid rather than as its diacyl parent. The frozen species set is given in Supplementary Table~S9.

\subsection*{Principal Component Analysis (PCA)}

Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed on individual lipid species within each experimental condition. Lipid intensities were first normalized to total ion current (TIC) to obtain relative abundances, then transformed using a centered log-ratio (CLR). The CLR-transformed data were mean-centered and scaled to unit variance, and PCA was conducted in R using the \emph{stats} package. The first two principal components were retained for visualization.

\subsection*{Genome-wide Association Studies (GWAS)}

GWAS analyses were carried out for each lipid trait under each condition using the mixed linear model (MLM) featured in GEMMA (v2.3) \citep{Zhou2012}. To address population structure and relatedness, a centered relatedness matrix (kinship) was computed from SNP genotype data. We filtered the genotype data by MAF 0.05 and heterozygosity of 0.8. For each lipid trait, the MLM was applied using the kinship matrix to handle population stratification effects. Besides individual traits, GWAS was also applied to summed lipid classes and all possible ratios. The first three genotype principal components were fitted as fixed covariates. A Bonferroni-corrected genome-wide significance threshold was employed to account for multiple comparisons. Based on approximately 6.11 million tested SNPs under CTL and 6.08 million under LIN, this corresponded to $p \le 8.19\times10^{-9}$ for CTL ($-\log_{10}(p)\ge 8.09$) and $p \le 8.22\times10^{-9}$ for LIN ($-\log_{10}(p)\ge 8.09$).

\subsection*{Botanical race and population-structure testing}

Botanical race, working group, and marker-based genetic cluster assignments for the SAP were taken from panel passport data. For race analysis, accessions were grouped into the five classical races (Bicolor, Caudatum, Durra, Guinea, Kafir) plus a Mixed group containing all hyphenated intermediate designations; accessions lacking a race assignment and the single \textit{S. bicolor} subsp. \textit{verticilliflorum} accession were excluded. The second grouping used the $K=6$ genetic cluster assignment. Fourteen traits were tested per condition: total lipid signal (log$_{10}$ of the summed SpATS-adjusted intensity across all annotated lipid features) and the \%TIC of each of 13 major lipid classes. Groups with fewer than five accessions in a condition were excluded from that test. Differences among groups were assessed with tie-corrected Kruskal--Wallis tests within each condition, and $P$-values were adjusted across the 14 traits per condition using the Benjamini--Hochberg procedure. Effect sizes were computed as $\varepsilon^2=(H-k+1)/(n-k)$, where $H$ is the tie-corrected statistic, $k$ the number of groups, and $n$ the number of accessions; negative values were truncated to zero. Because total ion current is partly technical, the total-lipid trait is reported for completeness and interpreted only relatively.

The stability of the class composition within a trial was assessed by resampling rather than by hypothesis testing. Neither procedure poses a null hypothesis, computes a test statistic, or yields a $P$-value. The first is a leave-one-group-out sensitivity analysis: within a trial, every accession belonging to one botanical race group was deleted and the mean \%TIC of each of the 13 lipid classes was recomputed from the remaining accessions, once for each of the six groups, giving 78 recomputations per trial. We report the smallest and largest value any omission produced and the largest absolute departure from the full-panel mean. The second is nonparametric subsampling: 1000 draws of 80\% of a trial's accessions were taken without replacement (315 of 394 under CTL, 290 of 363 under LIN, random seed fixed at 1), the same 13 class means were recomputed for each draw, and the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the resulting distribution are reported as a percentile interval. That interval describes how far the descriptive mean moves under resampling; it is not a confidence interval derived from a test, and it carries no significance claim. Both procedures run entirely within a single trial and involve no comparison between trials.

\subsection*{Candidate-locus definition and cross-trial overlap testing}

Cross-trial overlap was computed from the LD-mapped candidate tables, separately for the individual-lipid layer, the class-sum/ratio layer, and their union. At gene level, the background was the 34{,}027 annotated \textit{S. bicolor} v3.1 gene models. Because LD-based mapping assigns every gene within a window to one association, gene-level counts overstate independent signals, so we repeated the analysis at locus level. The genome was partitioned into non-overlapping 250~kb windows, and a window was a candidate locus for a condition if it contained at least one candidate gene from that condition. The locus-level background was the set of windows containing at least one annotated gene (2{,}279 windows). At both resolutions we assessed overlap with a one-sided hypergeometric (Fisher right-tail) test, and reported $P$-values with the fold enrichment over the expected overlap $|A||B|/N$ and the Jaccard index $|A\cap B|/|A\cup B|$, which is insensitive to set-size disparities between conditions.

For the per-class analysis, each candidate gene was assigned the lipid classes represented among the phenotypes with which it was significantly associated, parsed from the trait name and, where a species carried no class prefix, from the curated lipid class table. Because a single gene in the class-ratio layer can be associated with ratios spanning many classes, per-class overlap tests were stratified by trait layer. Class concordance was the proportion of genes recovered in both conditions that shared at least one lipid class between their CTL and LIN class sets.

\subsection*{Gene Annotation}

SNPs were aligned with the \textit{Sorghum bicolor} reference genome v3.1 (BTx623). Linkage disequilibrium in the sorghum association panel extends over appreciable physical distances \citep{Boatwright2022}, so a Bonferroni-significant SNP typically tags several linked genes rather than one. Significant SNPs were therefore grouped into association regions at $r^2 \geq 0.4$, a moderate cutoff that trades the narrow regions obtained at stringent thresholds, which discard genes tagged by imperfect proxies, against the inflated gene counts obtained at permissive ones. Because candidate-gene counts are considerably more sensitive to this cutoff than interval counts are, all interval-level analyses, including GO enrichment and the cross-trial overlap, were carried out on 250~kb intervals rather than on gene identifiers. Candidate genes were the annotated gene models falling within these LD-defined regions, with functional annotations and homology taken from Phytozome, SorghumBase, and TAIR for the corresponding \textit{Arabidopsis thaliana} orthologs. We aggregated the frequency of each candidate gene across all lipid GWAS findings and marked those with the highest recurrence.

\subsubsection*{Gene Ontology Enrichment}

GO biological-process and molecular-function terms were taken from the same PANTHER-derived annotation. The two ontologies were analyzed separately, each against a background restricted to sorghum genes carrying at least one annotation in that ontology. Over-representation was tested with a right-tailed hypergeometric test, retaining terms with at least two candidate genes in the stratum, and $p$-values were BH-corrected across all GO terms within each ontology and analysis. To account for LD among candidate genes, each term was also tested against a permutation null that preserved the candidate block structure. Permutation ran in two stages. Every term was screened with 2,000 draws, and any term with fewer than 20 hits in that screen was redrawn 100,000 times, which bounds the smallest attainable permutation $p$-value at $1.0\times10^{-5}$ and keeps the resolution below what BH correction across several hundred terms requires. The genome was divided into consecutive, non-overlapping 250~kb intervals, assigning each candidate gene to the interval containing its start, so the number of intervals carrying a term reflects the number of distinct genomic regions supporting that term rather than the number of gene IDs. Each permutation redrew the same number of intervals, each contributing the same number of genes, from background intervals matched on gene density. Permutation $p$-values were BH-corrected and reported as $q_{\mathrm{LD}}$.

Enrichment was computed separately within each lipid class, so that a term is attributed to the traits of one class rather than to the candidate set as a whole. Crossed with the two trait layers, the two ontologies and the two trials, this gives one analysis per class per layer per ontology per trial.

Because the Gene Ontology nests parent and child terms, a single candidate gene set can surface as several terms. Terms whose candidate gene sets were identical were therefore merged into one finding, keeping the most specific term as the label and retaining every merged term name and GO identifier in the supplementary table. The same gene set also recurs between analyses, since class sums and ratios share candidate genes by construction. Gene sets identical across analyses were merged in the same way, and the lipid classes in which each appeared were retained, because a gene set surfacing in a single class is class-specific whereas one surfacing across many is generic. After both merges, the 344 terms passing the LD-aware null reduced to 217 distinct candidate gene sets across 159 terms.

We retained gene sets whose GO term draws on at most 30 background genes. This is a criterion of specificity rather than of significance, applied because a term drawn from a background of hundreds or thousands of genes cannot be interpreted whatever its enrichment; \textit{nucleic acid binding} (970 background genes) and \textit{transmembrane transport} (865) both pass the permutation null without naming a lipid-related process. We additionally required at least two independent 250~kb intervals, because the permutation null draws gene-density-matched intervals from the genome, so a gene set confined to a single locus can pass it and still describe a place rather than a process; 16 such gene sets reach up to 324-fold enrichment. Of the 87 gene sets within the background limit, 71 meet the interval requirement, and the figure shows the four most enriched within each lipid class, giving 22. All 217 gene sets are listed in Supplementary Table~S23, with the uncollapsed per-term results in Supplementary Tables~S24 and S25, so nothing excluded from the figure is lost.

\subsection*{Genomic heritability}
Genomic heritability was estimated as
\[
h^2 = \frac{V_g}{V_g + V_e}
\]
from the model
\[
y = \mathbf{1}\mu + u + e,
\]
where
\[
u \sim N(0, K\sigma_g^2)
\]
and \(K\) is a genomic relationship matrix. The matrix \(K\) was built with PLINK~2 using \texttt{--make-rel square} from the SAP genotype set used for GWAS after LD pruning with \texttt{--indep-pairwise 50 5 0.2}, covering 400 genotypes. Variance components were estimated by REML using the eigendecomposition of \(K\), with the model reparameterised as \(V = \sigma^2\!\left(h^2 K + (1-h^2) I\right)\) so that \(h^2\) is profiled directly. The estimator was verified against \texttt{lme4} \citep{Bates2015lme4}, refitting the same model with \(K\) replaced by its symmetric square root so that the random coefficients are independent, which is the construction used internally when a relationship matrix is supplied to a mixed-model solver. Across the 34 class-sum traits the two agreed to within 0.0005 in \(h^2\) and 0.0011 in either interval bound, which is the resolution of the profile grid.

Intervals are 95\% profile-likelihood intervals, defined as the set of \(h^2\) values on a grid whose restricted log-likelihood falls within \(\chi^2_1/2\) of the maximum. A trait was called non-zero when this interval excluded zero. Bootstrap resampling was not used, as resampling genotypes with replacement creates pairs with relationship 1 and identical phenotypes, which forces the estimate to 1 in every replicate. For the per-species comparison, CTL and LIN were fitted using the same 351 genotypes and the same 164 species present in both trials, so the trials differed only in the phenotype. Total phenotypic variation per species was summarised as the coefficient of variation of its \%TIC across genotypes within a trial, and the two trials compared on those same species with a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test. The class sums were instead fitted on every accession and every species of each trial, on the summed spatially corrected intensity of the class transformed as \(\log_{10}(x+1)\), the same construction used for the per-species estimates and the usual scale for mass-spectrometry abundances. Because the trials differ in year, field, planting date and analytical batch and neither is replicated, all CTL--LIN comparisons are trial contrasts rather than effects of the nutrient treatment.

\subsection*{Lipid Metabolic Network Analysis (LINEX2)}

We used the Lipid Network Explorer (LINEX2; \url{https://exbio.wzw.tum.de/linex/}) to reconstruct annotation-guided lipid reaction networks and identify plausible biochemical connections among lipid classes and candidate enzymes. Lipid names were standardized to align with species notation (class plus acyl composition). LINEX2 constructs a global species network based on curated reaction rules, including headgroup interconversions, (de)acylation/editing, elongation, and desaturation, and maps these relationships onto the observed lipid abundance data. Since \textit{Sorghum bicolor} is not implemented as a default organism in LINEX2, we selected \textit{Oryza sativa} (OSA) as the reference species for network construction.

For branch-level analysis, we extracted candidate reactions linking DG/MG, TG, and lysophospholipid pools and summarized each branch using a reaction-balance score. Three were taken from the LINEX2 network. The fourth, an LCAT-like acyl-editing branch denoted LCAT*, was defined manually from reaction specificity (RHEA:32843, RHEA:44236) rather than retrieved from LINEX2, and the asterisk marks it as such throughout. For sample $i$ and lipid class $c$, let $C_{i,c}$ denote the class-sum abundance of class $c$ in sample $i$, let $\varepsilon_i$ denote the sample-specific pseudocount added prior to log transformation, and define $\ell_{i,c}=\log_{10}(C_{i,c}+\varepsilon_i)$ as the corresponding log-abundance. For each candidate reaction, we computed
\[
S_{i,\mathrm{rxn}}=\mathrm{mean}(\ell_{i,\mathrm{products}})-\mathrm{mean}(\ell_{i,\mathrm{substrates}}),
\]
so that larger $S_{i,\mathrm{rxn}}$ indicates a more product-weighted balance within that sample, whereas smaller values indicate a more substrate-weighted balance. The branch-specific scores were defined as:
\begin{itemize}
\item LCAT*: $S_i=\mathrm{mean}(\ell_{i,\mathrm{LPC}},\ell_{i,\mathrm{TG}})-\mathrm{mean}(\ell_{i,\mathrm{PC}},\ell_{i,\mathrm{DG}})$;
\item PNPLA1: $S_i=\ell_{i,\mathrm{DG}}-\ell_{i,\mathrm{TG}}$;
\item LRO1: $S_i=\mathrm{mean}(\ell_{i,\mathrm{TG}},\ell_{i,\mathrm{LPE}})-\mathrm{mean}(\ell_{i,\mathrm{PE}},\ell_{i,\mathrm{DG}})$;
\item PNPLA3: $S_i=\ell_{i,\mathrm{TG}}-\mathrm{mean}(\ell_{i,\mathrm{DG}},\ell_{i,\mathrm{MG}})$.
\end{itemize}
These scores were treated as branch-aligned summaries of lipid-pool balance rather than direct measures of enzymatic activity or metabolic flux. Reaction-balance scores were compared between CTL and LIN using paired genotype-matched Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, and $p$-values were adjusted using the Benjamini--Hochberg procedure.

\subsection*{Lipid Ontology Enrichment and Hierarchical Classification (LION/web)}

We conducted a functional ontology for lipids using LION/web \citep{Molenaar2019} (\url{http://www.lipidontology.com}). Lipid nomenclature was standardized according to LIPID MAPS annotations. The default settings of LION/web were applied for enrichment statistics and multiple-testing correction. We retained terms for $q \leq 0.10$. A hierarchical classification analysis of individual lipids and functional ontologies was also performed using LION/web.

Data processing and statistical analyses were performed in R (version 4.3.3). All the codes, figures, and pipeline are described in the GitHub repository: \url{https://github.com/rr-lab/SAP-Lipidomics-Database}.
